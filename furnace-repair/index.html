From the hallway, a furnace that will not heat looks the same no matter what broke. Inside the cabinet, though, everything happens in a strict order: inducer on, pressure switch closed, igniter glowing, gas valve open, burners lit, flame proven, and finally the blower moving heat through the ducts. Diagnosis is mostly a matter of finding where that chain breaks. In Conway homes, it tends to be the same handful of culprits: a flame sensor coated in oxide, a hot surface igniter cracked by thermal cycling, a pressure switch held open by a tired inducer or a plugged condensate trap, or a control board with a burned relay.

Our experienced technicians diagnose a furnace the way it operates, in order, measuring each stage as it happens. That means igniter resistance, microamp flame signal, pressure switch operation under actual inducer draft, gas valve voltage, and blower and capacitor condition. Safety checks are never optional on a gas appliance: heat exchanger inspection, leak testing on the gas connections, and flue draft verification, since a hazard rarely shows itself at the thermostat. We finish by checking temperature rise against the data plate, making sure the burner and blower work together the way the manufacturer designed them to.

Conway HVAC services natural gas, propane, and electric furnaces from all major brands, from standard 80 percent units to condensing high-efficiency models with their traps, drain lines, and dual pressure switches. Our trucks carry the parts that actually fail, such as igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors, so most homes have heat again before we leave. Warning Signs for Furnace Repair

Spotting the early warning signs gives you the chance to act before the situation spirals into something far more expensive. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Squealing or grinding from the blower compartment
  • Water, rust stains, or black soot around the flue or cabinet
  • The blower runs constantly but the air never warms
  • Frequent on-off cycling that never reaches the thermostat setting
  • The furnace clicks and glows but the burners never light
  • Burners light, run a few seconds, then shut down repeatedly
  • Loud booms at startup caused by delayed ignition
  • A pilot or burner flame that is lazy yellow instead of crisp blue

The Benefits of Professional Furnace Repair

The failure that matters most in a furnace is usually hidden. A cracked or corroded heat exchanger can let combustion gases into the airstream, and nothing about it is visible from the outside of the cabinet. Delayed ignition makes things worse, because each boom at light-off puts more stress on that metal. Add high voltage, a gas valve, and a flue to the same box, and there is little room for guesswork. Diagnosis is full of traps too. A high limit that keeps tripping could be a bad switch, or it could be doing exactly its job because a filter is clogged or the blower is dying, and replacing a good switch only hides the real fault.

Our experienced technicians treat every symptom as a question about the whole system. Flame signal gets measured rather than assumed, so a healthy sensor is never replaced while a cracked igniter or weak gas valve skates by. Draft is verified at the pressure switch and the flue. Temperature rise is checked against the data plate, which is how airflow problems get caught before they shorten the exchanger's life. And the gas side is leak-tested with instruments, not a sniff and a shrug. The result is a furnace that has been proven safe and correct under a full cycle, and an honest report if the unit is nearing the point where replacement beats another repair.

Furnace Repair Prevention Tips

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Make sure the furnace has clear combustion air; a packed utility room starves the burners
  • Put in a fresh filter monthly through the heating season, the cheapest protection your furnace can get
  • Clear boxes, lint, and above all chlorine-based cleaners out of the furnace area
  • Put a carbon monoxide detector on every level and test them monthly
  • Book a fall tune-up so weak igniters and fouled sensors are caught before the first freeze
  • Keep the condensate trap and drain clear on high-efficiency units to prevent lockouts
  • Pay attention when the burners light, since a boom instead of a soft whoosh calls for service
