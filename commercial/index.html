Kitchen Climate Control

Climate Control for Kitchens, Restaurants, and Food Facilities

In a restaurant, hood exhaust, makeup air, and dining-room comfort are all connected, and an undersized or failing system reveals itself as a sweltering cook line and complaints at the tables. Cooking lines dump enormous heat loads into the space, while the hoods pull conditioned air out as fast as the rooftop units can make it. We handle rooftop package units, split systems, tonnage and airflow sizing, and makeup air balancing, with service scheduled around your hours.

Comfort and air balance are critical issues for any food-service operator. A kitchen running negative pressure pulls dining-room air the wrong way, drags outdoor air through every door, and makes staff miserable during peak service. Our work keeps your system delivering the temperatures and airflow your operation depends on, with documented service records that show a well-maintained facility to landlords and auditors. You get detailed reports and service logs after each visit, along with the documentation many commercial leases require.

Replacement and maintenance plans are planned with owners, managers, and kitchen staff, so everything fits your operating schedule. If a job can't be done during service, we do it after close, keeping your kitchen cooking and the dining room comfortable throughout.

Inspections & Documentation

Safe Installations, Documented Service

Badly installed or neglected HVAC equipment can create carbon monoxide hazards, poor indoor air quality, refrigerant leaks, and costly water damage from clogged condensate lines. That's a real liability for any commercial property owner with employees, customers, and tenants in the building. Every installation and inspection we perform covers heat exchanger integrity, venting and combustion air, electrical connections and disconnects, refrigerant charge, condensate drainage, and ventilation rates for occupied spaces, followed by a written report that ranks each risk by priority and recommends specific fixes.

Regular professional service protects the people and property you're responsible for, and following through on our recommendations helps keep small issues from becoming emergencies. It also creates a record. We keep service logs, temperature and filter records, and a documentation binder for every account, with more frequent visits for heavy-use equipment in kitchens, gyms, and large mechanical rooms. When a landlord, insurer, or auditor asks for proof of due diligence, it's ready.

Emergency Service

Rapid-Response HVAC Repair for Businesses

A commercial HVAC failure starts costing money the minute it happens. Kitchens overheat, retail floors empty out, server rooms heat up, and tenants start calling. Calls to our commercial line get a technician rolling fast, throughout Conway and the surrounding area.

No two commercial emergencies look the same. It might be a failed compressor, a burned-out condenser fan motor, a refrigerant leak, a faulty ignition system or gas valve, a tripped high-limit switch, a frozen coil, or a control board stuck in lockout. Whatever it is, we find it fast, get the building comfortable again, and record every step for your service records. Then we put maintenance and monitoring in place so the same failure doesn't return.

Preventive Care

Scheduled HVAC Maintenance for Commercial Properties

A scheduled maintenance program from Conway HVAC is built around your property's specific equipment, how the space is used, and your budget. On each scheduled visit, we handle inspections, coil cleaning, condensate line clearing, filter changes at the right MERV rating, belt and bearing checks, refrigerant charge and static pressure testing, burner and heat exchanger inspection, and seasonal prep ahead of peak demand. It's the most cost-effective way to manage commercial HVAC equipment.

Scheduled maintenance typically costs a fraction of an emergency replacement and significantly reduces the risk of downtime and tenant complaints. Our commercial clients consistently report fewer outages, longer equipment life, and lower energy bills after implementing a program.
