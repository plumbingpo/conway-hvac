Think of a heat pump as an air conditioner that can run in reverse. In summer it pulls heat out of your home; in winter a reversing valve flips the refrigerant flow, and the same compressor draws heat from the outdoor air and brings it inside. Because it moves heat instead of creating it, a heat pump can deliver two to three times more heat energy than the electricity it uses, which is a big reason homeowners in Conway, AR are making the switch as older systems wear out. Today's cold-climate models, built around variable-speed inverter compressors, keep producing useful heat well below freezing, far beyond the heat pumps of twenty years ago.

Great heat pump performance starts with design work, and that is where Conway HVAC earns your trust. A heat pump has to be sized for two loads, summer cooling and winter heating, and the design has to find the balance point where the outdoor temperature meets the unit's capacity curve. Our technicians run the load calculation, compare single-stage, two-stage, and variable-speed inverter equipment against your climate and ductwork, and plan the backup heat properly, either electric strips staged below the balance point or a dual-fuel setup with a gas furnace that takes over in deep cold. We also check your electrical service and ducts, since heat pumps deliver lower supply temperatures than furnaces and need solid airflow to feel comfortable.

Installation follows the same discipline as every Conway HVAC system: line set pressure-tested and evacuated to a micron-verified vacuum, charge weighed in and confirmed with superheat and subcooling, the outdoor unit elevated on a stand where winter defrost drainage demands it, condensate handled properly, and the thermostat configured so staging, backup lockouts, and defrost behave the way the engineering intended. Then we commission in both modes, heating and cooling, and record the readings. Why Hire a Professional for Heat Pump Installation?

No residential system is less forgiving of shortcuts than a heat pump, since every error shows up in both seasons. A slightly wrong charge drags down summer efficiency and winter capacity alike. Maintenance Tips for Heat Pump Installation

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Expect light frost in winter and a periodic defrost hiss; solid ice means call us
  • Keep temperature settings steady so the backup strips are not needed to catch up
  • Check your first winter bill, since an unexpected jump often points to overworked backup heat
  • Plan two tune-ups a year so the system is verified in both heating and cooling
  • Give the outdoor unit open space all year, free of drifts and overgrowth
  • Rinse the outdoor coil each spring so summer performance starts clean
  • Replace filters on time, since restricted airflow hurts heat pump performance year-round
